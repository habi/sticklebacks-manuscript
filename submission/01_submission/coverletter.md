Dear Editors,

Herewith, we submit our manuscript, *High-Throughput Micro-CT and Automated Segmentation for Eco-Evolutionary Studies: A Case Study from Threespine Stickleback*, for consideration as an Original Article in *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*.

We developed a general, reproducible strategy for converting large multi-specimen μCT acquisitions into individually analyzable datasets, and demonstrate its utility using threespine stickleback.
By reducing the process to a series of straightforward image-processing steps, our approach enables the rapid and reproducible generation of individual tomographic datasets and can readily be adapted to other specimens and datasets.

We further demonstrate the utility of the workflow by applying automated segmentation to the branchial bones of the separated stickleback heads and by conducting an in-depth analysis of the ceratobranchial bones
These applications illustrate how the workflow can facilitate high-throughput morphological analyses while reducing manual processing effort.

The complete workflow is implemented as a set of Python scripts and is freely available online.
An anonymized version of the code is provided with the manuscript, allowing the methods to be readily reproduced and adapted to other tomographic datasets.

We believe that this work will be of interest to researchers seeking efficient, reproducible approaches for the analysis of large tomographic datasets in eco-evolutionary and comparative studies.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
We look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely,
David Haberthür
